Why we're doing this

How might we help older people feel less isolated and more connected to their communities?

Rooted Together tackles isolation and loneliness with a shared community allotment: a regular reason to get outside, a plot to care for and people to share it with. It is designed to improve mental wellbeing, encourage healthy lifestyles and bring younger and older generations together through gardening.

Our idea

Your own plot, and a community around it

We aim to secure a small piece of land in Durham [site to be confirmed] and divide it into individual growing plots for a first group of up to 20 members.

Each member grows whichever flowers, fruit or vegetables they choose, and looks after their own plot. That gives a regular reason to leave the house, spend time outdoors and work towards something meaningful.

It's about much more than gardening: a welcoming place to socialise, share skills, learn from one another and improve the local environment.

Our weekly programme

Two days a week, each with its own feel

Wednesdays: Social Gardening

A relaxed, informal afternoon to tend your plot and enjoy each other's company.

  • Gardening and looking after your plot
  • Music-sharing: swapping songs across generations
  • Shared picnics and refreshments
  • Time to chat and make friends

Sundays: Community and Skills

A more organised day for learning, sharing skills and helping local wildlife.

  • Gardening workshops and demonstrations
  • Older members teaching traditional growing skills
  • Students sharing sustainability know-how
  • Building bird feeders, hedgehog shelters and insect homes
  • Composting and recycling workshops

Through the seasons

  • SpringPlanting days, seed sharing and preparing plots.
  • SummerGardening, outdoor socials and environmental workshops.
  • AutumnHarvest celebrations and our community market.
  • WinterSeed library, planning next season and indoor workshops.

How it keeps going

Rooted Together will run as a community-focused social enterprise, funded by membership fees, paid workshops, harvest markets and festival activities, community grants and sponsorship, and partnerships with local organisations. Supported memberships keep it affordable.

Our first steps

Testing that it works and is affordable

  1. Secure land: approach Durham County Council, Durham University and local landowners.
  2. Check demand: talk to older residents, students and local families about joining and what they'd pay.
  3. Work out costs: land rental, insurance, water, tools and compost.
  4. Build partnerships with local gardening, environmental and community groups.
  5. Prepare the site and divide it into plots.
  6. Recruit our first 20 members, then launch Wednesday and Sunday sessions.

Before launching we'll also look into insurance, accessibility, land permissions and safeguarding.

Future plan

Garden Share

Later on, we'd like to match older people who can no longer manage their gardens with students and young people who want space to grow. Every first introduction would go through our team, and nobody's address or phone number would ever be shown.
